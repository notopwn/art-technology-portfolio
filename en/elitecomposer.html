FROM A BEAT TO AN ARRANGEMENT

From a single pattern
to an arrangement.

I wanted to build a native macOS app for making beats quickly and editing them visually. As I added a drum sequencer, piano roll, and sample editor, I connected the sounds made in each tool to a shared timeline.

01 / CREATE

Make the first step
easy to find.

Pattern creation was hidden behind a small + button. I brought it forward as “New Beat” and “New Melody”. Choose a name, length, and instrument or kit, open the editor, then use “Add to Timeline” when the pattern is ready.

Drums are edited as steps and velocities; melodies as pitches and durations. The source you create is separate from where you place it in the track.

RHYTHM

Every step, its own rhythm.

Adjust steps, velocity, and swing. Change kits while preserving the pattern, with an option to expand to eight pads.

MELODY

Draw pitch and duration.

Draw and select notes in the piano roll. Try nine instruments and sound controls, with settings saved for each pattern.

SAMPLES

New material from the original.

Trimming, reversing, fading, and slicing create new samples. The original and its existing clips stay intact.

02 / ARRANGE

Edit the pattern once.
Place each clip differently.

Multiple clips reference the same pattern. Editing that pattern updates its repeated clips, while each clip keeps its own position and length.

I aligned coordinate and snapping rules so a clip lands where its drag preview shows it. A completed move or adjustment can be reversed with one Undo.

03 / EDIT & MIX

Arrange. Edit.
Listen again.

When controls crowded the timeline or the mixer was hidden inside an editor, the workflow broke down. I put the arrangement at the center and gave Library, Inspector, Editor, and Mixer distinct roles.

INSPECT

Stay with the selected clip.

Check the selected clip’s position and length in the Inspector. Groups move together, while a double-click opens the source editor for the clip you clicked.

MIX

A mixer when you need it.

Switch between Editor and Mixer in the lower panel, then close it to return to the arrangement. Opening a panel is kept separate from the history of musical edits.

AUTOMATE

Sound that changes over time.

Record volume and pan as points on the timeline. Values interpolate between points, changing a sound's level and stereo position across the track.

SAVE THE WORK. SHARE THE SOUND.

Save the track.
Pick it up again.

Save patterns, samples, arrangement, and workspace state in the project, then export a mix or individual track stems. I compared rendered audio before and after saving, and against exports, to check that the work carried beyond the screen.
